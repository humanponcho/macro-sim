# Reconciling the two chains

The teaching material describes the economy twice. The masterclass and the full
lesson pack use **ten layers**. The beginner lesson uses a **thirteen-step
chain**. They describe the same machine, but a class that sees both numbering
schemes at once will get confused about which layer is which.

The app uses the ten-layer model everywhere. This file maps the beginner chain
onto it, so a student holding either handout sees the same picture on screen.

## The mapping

| Beginner step | Ten-layer home | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Demographics and geography | **L1** Demographics and geography | Direct match. |
| 2. Natural resources | **L2** Energy and natural resources | The beginner chain splits resources from energy. The ten-layer model keeps them together, because they are one physical constraint. |
| 3. Energy | **L2** Energy and natural resources | Merged with step 2. |
| 4. Production and productivity | **L3** Production and supply chains | Direct match. Productivity lives here in both. |
| 5. Global trade and supply chains | **L4** International trade | Direct match. |
| 6. Income, consumption, saving, investment | **L5** and **L6** | This is the one step that genuinely splits. Income and consumption are L5. Saving and investment are L6, because that is where the financial system takes over. |
| 7. GDP and economic growth | **L5** Labour, income and consumption | Growth is the *result* of L5, not a layer of its own. |
| 8. Inflation and employment | **L5**, feeding **L7** | Inflation and employment are outcomes of the real economy that the central bank then reacts to. They are the pressure gauge, not a floor of the building. |
| 9. Government and fiscal policy | *not a layer* | See below. |
| 9. Banks and credit | **L6** Savings, credit and investment | Direct match. |
| 10. Central banks, interest rates, financial conditions | **L7** Central banks and interest rates | Direct match. |
| 11. Government bonds and currencies | **L8** The US dollar and government bonds | Direct match. |
| 12. Equities, corporate bonds, property, commodities, gold | **L9** Asset markets | Direct match. |
| 13. Wealth, confidence, spending | **L10** The feedback loop | Direct match. |

## The three differences worth explaining out loud

**Energy and resources are one layer, not two.** The beginner chain separates
them. Splitting them suggests they behave differently, and they do not: both are
physical inputs that constrain everything above.

**GDP, inflation and employment are readings, not floors.** The beginner chain
gives them their own steps, which makes them look like places a shock passes
through. They are better taught as the gauges the machine produces, and the
gauges the central bank reads before acting.

**Fiscal policy has no layer.** The beginner chain gives government spending and
taxation a step of its own, alongside banks and credit. The ten-layer model does
not, and neither does the engine. Fiscal policy is real and it matters, but the
specification rules it out of version 1, and adding it would mean adding a whole
block of variables the model was designed to do without.

Say this to a class rather than hiding it: *"Governments tax and spend, and that
changes the economy. This model does not include it, so when we talk about
government spending today we will talk in words, not numbers."*

## What to say if a student notices

They are right, and it is a good thing to notice. The honest answer is that a
map is a choice about what to leave out. The ten-layer version leaves out fewer
things than the thirteen-step version appears to, because the thirteen-step
version counts the same physical constraint twice and counts three gauges as
layers.

Neither is wrong. One is easier to trace a shock through, and tracing a shock is
the whole point of the exercise.
